Nothing about the circle changes. Where several lines call at one coordinate, the circles step off the point onto a small ring and a leader ties each back to the station pin, so a reader still reads area as boardings and still reads fill as which line.

The pin, not the circle, is the station Each circle sits about one radius off the true coordinate. At 7th Street that displacement is 22 px, which is roughly 150 m at zoom 12.
It costs the one thing a map is for A fan keeps every encoding the app already argues for and asks the reader to learn nothing, which is why it is worth drawing first. What it spends is position, and position is the only channel a map has that a table does not. The four-line case above shows the limit: 7th Street's fan spans 86 px and reaches Pershing Square, so a displacement small enough to stay honest is too small to separate the circles, and one large enough to separate them puts a mark on the wrong block. Fixing that means the fan opens only for the station a reader has selected, which makes it an interaction rather than an encoding.
