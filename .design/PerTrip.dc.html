FF Boardings per trip is a Stop Measure, not a layer
Click the floor. Every figure is the real bus payload joined to the real timetable.
Dividing a stop’s Boardings by the trips that call there answers a question Boardings alone cannot: whether a quiet stop is quiet because nobody wants it or because almost nothing stops. It selects a figure rather than filtering anything, which is exactly what a Stop Measure does, so it belongs beside Boardings and Alightings in the same control rather than in the layer picker.

How far the ranking actually moves Of the hundred busiest bus stop-and-line pairs, the median moves 43 places when the measure changes, the ninetieth percentile moves 274, and one moves 598. The two measures are not a rescaling of each other, which is what makes this worth a control.
Why rail gets almost nothing from it Every stop on a rail line sees the same number of trains, so within a line the new measure divides by a constant and reorders nothing. It separates the six rail lines from each other and stops there. On bus the divisor runs from 18 trips a day to 243 across the middle nine tenths of stops.
